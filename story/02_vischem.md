## OVERVIEW
Vischem does not make drugs. It does not make devices. It makes the parts that make the parts that save lives — microfluidic chips, bioreactor membranes, precision-cast surgical instrument components, pharmaceutical-grade polymer compounds. If a hospital is running dialysis, a lab is culturing cells, or a factory is filling vaccine vials, there is a reasonable chance a Vischem component is somewhere in that chain.

This invisibility is not accidental. It is the company's greatest strategic asset — and, from 2011 onward, the Covenant Group's most important protection.

---

## HISTORY

### FOUNDING ERA (1987–1999)
Ernst Vischer, a polymer chemist from Basel with no particular political instincts and a genuine passion for materials science, founded Vischem GmbH in 1987 with twelve employees and a single contract supplying tubing to a Zurich-based dialysis manufacturer. The company grew slowly and honestly through the 1990s — acquiring small competitors, expanding into bioreactor components, building a reputation for precision that was difficult to fault and impossible to sensationalise.

Ernst Vischer died in 2004, having spent his career making things no one noticed and being quietly proud of that. He had no idea what his company would become.

### EXPANSION ERA (1999–2010)
The late 1990s pivot into biochemical compounds — adding "Integrated BioSystems" to the name — was driven by Ernst's son, **Markus Vischer**, who recognised that the pharmaceutical manufacturing boom required not just physical components but chemical substrates. Vischem's polymer expertise translated directly into pharmaceutical-grade lipid compounds, cell culture media, and diagnostic reagents. By 2005, Vischem was supplying to over 200 pharmaceutical manufacturers across 40 countries. By 2010, that number had doubled.

Markus was a competent executive and a weak patriarch. He restructured the Vischer Family Trust in 2008 in a way that brought in outside board members to manage the investment portfolio — a decision he made for liquidity reasons and would, had he lived to see 2022, have regretted entirely.

Markus Vischer died of a cardiac event in 2017. His daughter Maren had already been appointed CEO the previous year.

### CO-OPTION (2011 ONWARD)
In 2011 the Covenant Group identified Vischem as its ideal vehicle. The co-option was not hostile. It was patient, structural, and invisible.

The mechanism: Covenant-connected sovereign wealth vehicles in Luxembourg and the Netherlands quietly accumulated minority positions in the Vischer Family Trust's investment portfolio over three years. Nothing controlling. Nothing alarming. Just enough to place one person — **The Vischer Insider** — onto the Trust's board in 2014 with full visibility into Vischem's research allocation decisions.

From that position, Alivar Lifesciences — the Covenant Group's Luxembourg-registered front foundation — began directing an annual research allocation to Vischem's advanced biomaterials division in 2013. The sums were not large enough to attract attention. They were large enough to fund the lipid nanoparticle delivery platform that would eventually become VIB-C19T.

By 2016, the platform had a name, a Bengaluru facility to develop it in, and a CEO who believed entirely in its therapeutic potential. Dr. Maren Vischer did not know about SOLVANE. She did not know about the Covenant Group. She knew she had a promising delivery platform, a supportive board, and a pandemic that had given her an opportunity to do something genuinely important.

That sincerity was the best protection the Covenant Group had.

## KEY PERSONNEL

### DR. MAREN VISCHER — CEO
**Age:** 47
**Background:** MD and biochemistry doctorate from ETH Zurich. Completed a postdoctoral fellowship at the Karolinska Institute in Stockholm before returning to Basel to join Vischem's research division in 2003. Appointed CEO 2018.

**Character:** Precise, driven, and genuinely idealistic in the way that people are when they have never had to separate their personal ambitions from their professional ones. Speaks four languages. Gives carefully prepared public statements that manage to be both transparent and entirely uninformative. Runs every morning at 5:30 AM regardless of timezone.

**Role in the story:** Maren is not a villain. She is the Covenant Group's most valuable asset specifically because she is not a villain. A CEO who genuinely believes in the company's therapeutic mission, who fights for its regulatory approvals, who weeps — privately, once, in a Basel hotel bathroom — when she reads the Kerala death toll, is a CEO whose sincerity no investigator can easily puncture.

The game never gives the player a scene where Maren discovers the truth. That absence is intentional. Whether she eventually finds out is a question the game leaves open. The implication is that the Covenant Group has no intention of ever letting her.

**Public persona:** Measured, credible, occasionally visionary. Her 2021 press statement after the DCGI clearance — "India is not simply a manufacturing base for us. It is a centre of scientific excellence" — was widely quoted. It was also entirely true from her perspective, which is why it landed.

### HENRIK SAAL — CHIEF COMMUNICATIONS OFFICER
**Age:** 52
**Background:** Not a scientist. Former EU pharmaceutical lobby strategist, Brussels and Strasbourg, 2001–2015. Recruited by Vischem in 2015 specifically for his ability to manage regulatory narratives.

**Character:** Calm in the way that people are calm when they have spent twenty years in rooms where terrible things were being said in very measured tones. Has no ideology. Has no agenda beyond the protection of the institutional entity he is paid to represent. Considers himself a professional, not a participant.

**Role in the story:** Every Vischem public statement after the March 2022 incident passes through Henrik Saal. Every carefully worded non-denial, every expression of "full cooperation," every framing of the SOLVANE leak as "fabricated or selectively misrepresented" — that is Saal's work. He is not lying, technically, in any single statement. He is constructing a corridor of language that leads nowhere.

He reports to The Vischer Insider before he reports to Maren Vischer. Maren does not know this.

### DR. PRIYA ANAND — HEAD OF VISCHEM INDIA OPERATIONS
**Age:** 39
**Background:** Born in Chennai. Biochemistry undergraduate at IIT Madras. PhD at Imperial College London. Returned to India in 2014, joined Vischem's Bengaluru facility in 2016 as a senior researcher. Promoted to Head of India Operations in 2020.

**Character:** Brilliant and ambitious in equal measure. Came back to India because she wanted to build something here, not manage a satellite office of someone else's ambition. VIB-C19T was her project in every meaningful sense — she drove the Phase I results, she pushed for the Phase II expansion, she made the internal case for the high-dosage cohort in January 2022.

Not evil. Not corrupt in any simple financial sense. Just someone who wanted the project to succeed badly enough to stop looking too closely at the data anomalies in the high-dosage cohort's preliminary results. There were anomalies. She reclassified them as within acceptable variance. That decision, made on a Tuesday afternoon in a Bengaluru office, is the proximate human cause of B.1.VIB.

**Role in the story:** Dr. Priya Anand does not appear directly in the game. She appears in documents — internal emails, trial reports, the DCGI submission — that Siddharth uncovers during his investigation sections. She is the human face of how institutional ambition becomes catastrophe. By the time the game begins she has resigned from Vischem and her current whereabouts are unknown. Whether MERIDIAN has found her first is a detail the game leaves deliberately ambiguous.

## THE SCIENCE — VIB-C19T AND B.1.VIB

### VIB-C19T
A lipid nanoparticle delivery compound designed to deliver a synthetic immunomodulatory peptide sequence directly to lung tissue. Intended to intercept and suppress the cytokine cascade in moderate-to-severe COVID-19 patients before it became fatal.

Not a vaccine. A post-infection therapeutic.

Its legitimate therapeutic application was real and potentially significant. Phase I and early Phase II results were genuinely promising. The compound, at standard doses, did what it was designed to do.

At high doses — the January 2022 cohort — it did not merely suppress the immune response. It partially disabled the body's natural viral clearance mechanisms, creating a prolonged high-viral-load environment inside trial participants. SARS-CoV-2, given pressure-free conditions and a chemically neutralised immune system, evolved.

### B.1.VIB
The variant that emerged from the high-dosage cohort. Internally designated B.1.VIB by INSACOG researchers.

**Key characteristics:**
- Carries spike protein mutations not seen in any prior catalogued strain
- Modified furin cleavage site enhances ability to infect lower respiratory tissue more rapidly than Omicron
- Symptom profile: deceptively mild for first 72 hours, followed by abrupt multi-organ deterioration
- The 72-hour mild window makes community detection and isolation extremely difficult — infected individuals feel well enough to move, travel, and transmit

**What the 72-hour window means for the story:**
Every character the player encounters in Kerala could be three days from collapse. The game does not make this explicit in its mechanics but builds it into environmental storytelling — NPCs who seem fine, background radio reports of sudden hospitalisations, the particular anxiety of a population that has learned not to trust how it feels.

## THE DUAL PURPOSE — WHAT VIB-C19T REALLY WAS

The lipid nanoparticle platform was not designed as a COVID therapeutic. It was designed as a **nutritional payload delivery mechanism** — a system for delivering precise synthetic micronutrient profiles directly to gut and lung tissue, bypassing the variable absorption rates of natural food digestion.

The COVID application was the cover. The pandemic provided the regulatory environment — compressed timelines, emergency approvals, a population primed to accept novel interventions — that allowed a nutritional delivery platform to be field-tested as a therapeutic. The data SOLVANE needed was not efficacy data. It was absorption data, distribution data, and — in the case of the high-dosage cohort — mutation data. How does a novel pathogen behave inside a human population whose immune clearance mechanisms have been chemically modified? You cannot answer that question in a laboratory.

B.1.VIB was, in the coldest reading of SOLVANE's objectives, a data point. An expensive, uncontrolled, globally consequential data point. But a data point.

## VISCHEM'S SUPPLEMENT ENDGAME

The Covenant Group's long-term objective, for which Vischem is the primary instrument:

**Phase 1 — Delivery platform validation** (complete by 2022, at catastrophic cost)
Confirm that the lipid nanoparticle system can deliver nutritional payloads to human tissue effectively at population scale.

**Phase 2 — Food supply degradation** (ongoing)
Through Alivar Lifesciences-funded policy research, lobbying, and sympathetic academic output, accelerate the degradation of traditional agricultural supply chains in target markets. Make natural food systems appear unreliable, unscalable, and unsafe. Kerala's spice and rice agriculture, already under pressure from urbanisation and climate instability, is a primary target.

**Phase 3 — Supplement introduction** (planned 2023 onward)
Following the B.1.VIB outbreak — which has traumatised Kerala's immune landscape, destabilised its food supply chains, and produced a population with compromised natural nutritional absorption — introduce synthetic nutritional supplements through legitimate commercial channels. The supplements work. The health outcomes are measurable. The population feels better.

**Phase 4 — Dependency consolidation** (indefinite)
By the time anyone understands what happened to their food autonomy, the dependency will have been in place long enough that dismantling it feels more dangerous than maintaining it.

## DESIGN LANGUAGE

### Philosophy
Vischem's visual identity was designed by people who consider aesthetics a form of argument. Clean, cold, and authoritative — the design language of an institution that has decided it does not need to be liked, only trusted.

### Logo
A single unbroken horizontal line bisected by a minimal hexagonal form — referencing molecular structure, pipeline conduit, and capsule cross-section simultaneously. Three meanings. Zero explanation offered. The line extends past the hexagon on both sides — Vischem is a conduit. Something passes through them. White on black, always.

### Wordmark
"Vischem" bold, "Integrated Biosystems." regular weight. The period at the end of "Integrated Biosystems." is intentional. The matter is settled. No further questions.

### Typography
- Primary: Grotesque sans-serif, ultra light weight for headlines. Cold, precise, extensive negative space.
- Secondary: Monospaced typeface for all data, figures, technical documentation. Facts, not feelings.
- Body copy set small and dense — comprehensive rather than readable. Vischem's information is for professionals, not the public.

### Imagery
- No people in official communications. Only materials, surfaces, and processes.
- Extreme close-ups of compound structures, membrane cross-sections, fluid dynamics.
- When people appear — annual reports, investor decks — they are always in PPE. Faces partially obscured.
- High contrast black and white only. No gradients. No warmth. Nothing that reads as organic.

### Architecture
- Facilities deliberately understated externally. No visible signage from roads. No branded facades.
- Interiors: poured concrete, matte black fixtures, white walls, no art.
- The Bengaluru facility on Hosur Road looks from the outside like a mid-tier logistics warehouse. Intentional.
- Basel HQ public reception: one white desk, one black chair, logo etched directly into concrete wall. Nothing else.

### The Feeling
Vischem's design language should make the viewer feel they are looking at something built to outlast them — indifferent to their reaction, confident in its own permanence. Not threatening. Just completely, utterly unconcerned with whether anyone finds it welcoming.

## VISCHEM IN THE GAME

### Locations
- **Vischem Bengaluru Facility** — seen only in flashback sequences and recovered documents. The origin point of everything.
- **Vischem Kochi Port Facility** — the game's primary Vischem physical presence in Kerala. A logistics and distribution hub established in late 2021, originally for supplement distribution infrastructure. Now doubling as a private security operations base. The climax location.
- **Vischem Kerala Distribution Network** — a series of smaller nodes across Ernakulam, Thrissur, and Thiruvananthapuram that appear as environmental locations throughout the game.

### Vischem Private Security
Following the March 2022 incident and the contract suspension, Vischem did not withdraw from India. It redeployed. Its private security contractor — a Luxembourg-registered firm called **Struker Protective Services** — expanded its Kerala presence significantly from April 2022 onward, ostensibly to protect company assets during the unrest. Actually to manage the supplement distribution infrastructure and suppress any investigation that gets too close.

Struker operatives are the game's primary combat enemy. They are not soldiers. They are not ideologues. They are contractors doing a job, which makes them efficient and makes them dangerous and makes them entirely expendable to the people who hired them.

### Documents and Collectibles
Vischem facilities are the primary source of the game's investigation collectibles — internal emails, procurement records, trial data, SOLVANE project files. Each Vischem location contains a layer of legitimate company documentation on top and Covenant Group operational material underneath. Siddharth's investigation chapters are built around peeling those layers apart.
