# 05 — Asterisk Incorporated
*Last updated: 2022 story bible, pre-production draft*

---

## FULL NAME
**Asterisk Incorporated**
Registered: Luxembourg, 2016
Operational HQ: Houston, Texas, USA
Secondary offices: Bangalore, India | Tokyo, Japan | Amsterdam, Netherlands
Lunar Operations Base: Shackleton Crater Rim, Lunar South Pole (established 2021)
Mars Research Outpost: Hellas Planitia Basin (established 2022, three-person crew)

---

## TAGLINE (PUBLIC)
*"Resources for the next era."*

---

## OVERVIEW
Asterisk Incorporated is the world's leading private off-world materials extraction and resource prospecting company. It is what SpaceX would be if SpaceX was not interested in taking people to space — only in what space contains and what it is worth.

Publicly: a Luxembourg-incorporated megacorp with a majority ownership consortium of American, Japanese, and Indian sovereign wealth vehicles, an operational partnership with ISRO's commercial arm, and a rapidly growing portfolio of off-world extraction patents, prospecting licences, and resource claims across near-Earth asteroids, the lunar surface, and Mars.

Privately: the entity whose 2019 lunar south pole finding changed the Covenant Group's timeline and, by chain of consequence, produced B.1.VIB, destabilised Kerala, and set the events of this game in motion.

Asterisk did not intend any of that. It is not certain Asterisk knows any of that. What it found, and what that finding means for everything that follows, is the largest unanswered question in the game's lore — and possibly the most important one.

## HISTORY

### FORMATION (2010–2016)
Asterisk began as a Luxembourg-registered consortium of materials scientists, aerospace engineers, and sovereign wealth managers who had independently arrived at the same conclusion: the clean energy transition's demand for critical minerals — lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earth elements, platinum group metals — was going to outpace Earth's extractable supply within two generations, and whoever built the infrastructure to source those minerals off-world first would hold the dominant position in the global economy for the century that followed.

The consortium spent six years raising capital, acquiring launch partnerships, and lobbying for the legal frameworks — in Luxembourg, the United States, Japan, and the UAE — that would give private companies the right to own and sell resources extracted from celestial bodies. This was patient, unglamorous work. Asterisk did not become publicly visible until its formal incorporation in 2016.

The India connection was established early. ISRO's commercial arm — newly aggressive in the post-2014 expansion of India's space programme — was Asterisk's first major governmental partner. The arrangement gave Asterisk access to ISRO's launch infrastructure and ground support networks. It gave India a seat at the table of the off-world resource economy at a moment when China was building its own parallel programme and the United States was moving to consolidate its own first-mover advantage.

That Indian seat at Asterisk's table is the specific thing the Covenant Group identified as the threat that could not be permitted to mature.

### OPERATIONAL EXPANSION (2016–2019)
Asterisk's first prospecting missions were robotic — a series of increasingly capable autonomous prospectors sent to near-Earth asteroids and the lunar surface between 2017 and 2019. The public results of these missions were released in summary form and were impressive but unsurprising: confirmation of water ice deposits, rare earth element concentrations, platinum group metal signatures consistent with prior remote sensing data.

The private results of the 2019 lunar south pole mission were not released. They have not been released as of the game's 2022 setting. What was found is known, within Asterisk, to approximately fourteen people. Outside Asterisk, it is known — through The Intelligence Man's networks — to the Covenant Group.

### THE MOON AND MARS (2020–2022)
Asterisk's first crewed lunar mission landed at Shackleton Crater rim in March 2021. A three-person crew spent forty-seven days establishing the Lunar Operations Base — a pressurised habitat, power generation array, and preliminary extraction equipment — before returning to Earth. A second crew rotated in September 2021 and has been in continuous residence since.

The Mars Research Outpost at Hellas Planitia Basin was established by an Asterisk-ISRO joint crew in January 2022 — three people, a twelve-month initial rotation, robotic support. It is the smallest permanently crewed outpost on another planet in human history. It is also, by any measure, the most significant thing humanity has done since the Apollo programme.

The game's world does not make a large public event of this. By 2022 in this timeline, a small Mars outpost is remarkable but no longer unimaginable. The acceleration of space exploration has compressed the emotional register of these achievements. People on Earth are aware that humans are on Mars. Most of them are thinking about something else.

## WHAT ASTERISK FOUND

This section documents what is known, what is inferred, and what remains unknown within the game's lore. It is intentionally incomplete. The game does not fully resolve this question.

### What Is Known
The 2019 robotic prospecting mission to the lunar south pole — specifically the permanently shadowed regions of Shackleton Crater — returned data that caused Asterisk's senior leadership to classify the mission results and convene an emergency board meeting within seventy-two hours of the data arriving on Earth.

The classification was not lifted. The board meeting produced a decision to accelerate the crewed lunar programme by eighteen months and to significantly expand the scope of the Mars mission beyond its original prospecting brief.

### What Is Inferred
The permanently shadowed regions of the lunar south pole are known to contain water ice. Water ice at extractable concentrations was expected and would not have triggered emergency classification. What triggered classification was something else — something in the data that changed Asterisk's understanding of what it was doing and why it needed to move faster.

The leading inference — held by MERIDIAN's analysts, never confirmed — is that the 2019 data revealed concentrations of Helium-3 in the lunar regolith at the south pole that were significantly higher than any prior model had projected. Helium-3 is extraordinarily rare on Earth. On the Moon, implanted by four billion years of solar wind bombardment, it exists in quantities that could — with extraction and fusion energy technology currently at prototype stage — supply Earth's entire energy demand for centuries.

Whoever controls that supply controls the energy future of the planet. Not through force. Through the simple fact of possession.

### What The Covenant Group Understood
When The Intelligence Man's networks surfaced the Asterisk classification decision and its probable cause in late 2019, The Successor understood immediately what it meant for the Covenant Group's programme.

A world moving toward Helium-3 fusion energy — with India as a primary partner in the extraction operation — is a world in which India's long-term strategic and economic trajectory moves decisively away from the dependency conditions the Covenant Group was engineering. An energy-independent, space-resource-connected India does not need synthetic nutrition supplements from a European supply chain. It does not need any of the things the Covenant Group was positioning itself to provide.

The window was not closing. It had already started to close. The Successor moved immediately.

### What Remains Unknown
Whether the Helium-3 inference is correct. Whether Asterisk found something else entirely. Whether what they found on the Moon connects to what the Mars outpost is looking for. Whether MERIDIAN knows more than its analysts have documented.

The game leaves these questions open. They are the foundation for whatever comes after this story.

## ASTERISK AND INDIA

The ISRO partnership is the most strategically significant relationship in Asterisk's operational structure and the most consequential element of its presence in this lore.

India's rapid space expansion in this timeline — accelerated beyond the real-world trajectory by a combination of political will, private investment, and the competitive pressure of China's parallel programme — gave ISRO's commercial arm the launch capability and ground infrastructure that Asterisk needed and the geopolitical credibility that its Luxembourg registration could not provide.

In exchange, India received:
- A significant equity stake in Asterisk's resource revenue streams
- Priority access to off-world extracted materials for domestic manufacturing
- Three Indian nationals in the current Mars outpost crew rotation
- A seat on Asterisk's board that carries genuine decision-making weight

This is not charity. Asterisk's founders understood that a company extracting resources from space needed governmental legitimacy that purely Western ownership could not provide in an increasingly multipolar world. India was the right partner at the right moment — large enough to matter, technically capable enough to contribute, and politically positioned as a non-aligned player that neither the American nor Chinese space programmes could easily absorb.

The Covenant Group's reading of this arrangement: if India's Asterisk stake matures into genuine off-world resource access, the population dependency programme becomes irrelevant for the world's most populous nation. Every year India remains stable and its Asterisk partnership develops, the window narrows further.

## ASTERISK'S RELATIONSHIP TO MERIDIAN

Not confirmed. Not denied. Inferable.

The resources required to maintain MERIDIAN's decade-long intelligence operation are not available to private individuals. The entity with the most direct strategic interest in the Covenant Group's failure is Asterisk. The connection between them — if it exists — is the most closely held secret in either organisation.

If the connection is real, it means the following: the three protagonists, who believe they are acting to stop a pharmaceutical conspiracy and save Kerala, are also — without knowing it — instruments of a space mining megacorp's geopolitical strategy. They are not wrong to do what they are doing. Their goals and Asterisk's goals align completely in the context of 2022 Kerala.

Whether they would still act if they knew is a question the game does not ask them. Zara has considered it. She has decided it does not change the operational calculus. She may be right.

## ASTERISK IN THE GAME

### Direct Presence
Asterisk does not appear directly in the game's Kerala setting. It exists in the game through:

- **Environmental storytelling** — news fragments, radio broadcasts, background screens showing Asterisk mission updates, the Mars outpost crew rotation
- **Documents** — Siddharth's investigation chapters surface financial connections between Asterisk's Luxembourg holding structure and MERIDIAN-adjacent entities
- **Zara's knowledge** — she knows more about Asterisk than she discloses. Several of her dialogue lines, re-examined after the game's final reveal, carry a different weight.
- **The ending** — the final chapter's post-credit sequence contains a single image and a single line of text that reframes Asterisk's role in everything that has happened. It does not explain. It implies.

### What The Player Understands by the End
That the Covenant Group and Asterisk are engaged in a conflict whose real stakes — the energy future of the planet, the question of whether humanity goes to space free or carrying its chains — are so much larger than Kerala that the protagonists were never going to be able to see the whole board. They saw their corner of it. They acted on what they saw. That was enough. Whether it was enough for the right reasons is the question they will have to live with.

## DESIGN LANGUAGE

### Philosophy
Asterisk's visual identity is the most deliberately deceptive of the three faction aesthetics. It is designed to look like a technology company that wants to change the world for the better — approachable, optimistic, human-scaled. This is intentional misdirection. Asterisk is extracting the resources of celestial bodies for private profit. The warm blue gradient and the friendly pinwheel mark are the aesthetic equivalent of the Covenant Group's supplements — something that feels good and functions as intended and contains, in its structure, a power relationship the consumer was never invited to examine.

### Colour
Warm blue gradient — sky at the top, deeper ocean blue at the base. The colour of possibility, of open horizons, of the kind of future that technology companies have been selling since the 1990s. Against Vischem's black and MERIDIAN's blood-red, Asterisk's blue reads as the benign option. It is not the benign option. It is simply the most palatable one.

### Logo Mark
The asterisk/pinwheel form — six asymmetric white petals radiating from a central point — reads simultaneously as a star, a propeller, a molecular bond, and a loading spinner. Dynamic. Forward-moving. The kind of mark that suggests things are in motion, that progress is happening, that you are in capable hands. The asymmetry is subtle enough to read as intentional design rather than error — it gives the mark energy without instability.

### Wordmark
"Asterisk" bold, "Incorporated" regular weight — the same mixed-weight convention as Vischem's wordmark, but where Vischem's period makes a full stop, Asterisk's open ending trails off into possibility. Centred, optimistic, slightly larger than it needs to be. Confident without being aggressive.

### The Feeling
Asterisk's design should make you feel like you are looking at the company that is going to save us. Clean energy, off-world resources, the end of terrestrial scarcity. That feeling is not entirely wrong. Asterisk may genuinely do those things. The question the design does not ask — and is very carefully structured not to ask — is who "us" refers to, and on whose terms the saving happens.
